7. Conclusions

The public catalogue record contains a preferred resident sector and a persistent comet-aphelion dipole under the tested angular nulls. These are the strongest reasons to investigate a common spatial structure in the outer Solar System. Historical cometary rotation contrasts add information about the selected population, but the rotations themselves are reproduced by standard planetary dynamics and the equivalent-time conversion is not a proper-time measurement.

The temporal rotation holdout fails the original fixed-sector excess prediction. The later displaced structure is sensitive to astrometric-catalogue composition, while corrected mirror and meridian comparisons remain exploratory. Overlap among catalogues, directions from the same orbit, and shared measurement procedures precludes counting the reported channels as independent proofs.

TEP offers a possible organizing hypothesis whose value would lie in predicting these channels jointly from a specified field and matter response. The present results motivate that test but do not yet establish a temporal domain boundary or a preference for TEP over a calibrated conventional model. The decisive advance is a successful, frozen prediction of new observations through the same survey-selection and orbit-fitting procedures used to evaluate the alternatives.
