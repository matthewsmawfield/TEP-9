3. The resident signature: spatial alignment

3.1 The measured concentration

The secure detached sample has $R_\varpi =$ {{claim:resident_r}} and mean longitude $49.1^\circ$, with a 68 per cent bootstrap interval of $33.2^\circ$–$66.6^\circ$ (step 010). Its conditioned angular-null probability is {{claim:resident_p}}. This is evidence against that specified reference distribution. The estimated direction and its uncertainty come from the same objects whose clustering is tested; subsequent axis-conditioned resident statistics are not fresh replications.

The extreme subset also concentrates in the same broad sector, but its smaller membership gives weaker evidence. The five-object high-perihelion subset is not significant under its corresponding conditioned null. Scans over detachment thresholds, angular cap sizes and orbital quality are useful sensitivity checks; nested cuts are highly correlated and their count cannot be interpreted as a count of independent detections (steps 023, 025 and 111).

3.2 What the footprint tests establish

The discovery-footprint analysis measures a proxy for each object's discovery longitude and explores its coupling to $\varpi$ (steps 014–016). Setting perihelion longitude equal to that proxy gives a resultant of 0.295, below the observed 0.332. This deterministic realization is not a universal ceiling on discovery bias: finite random scatter can exceed a mean attenuation curve, and object-dependent coupling, magnitude, latitude, tracking and quality selection can all alter the distribution. A proper bound requires the full relevant sampling process, with its tail probability.

The footprint-conditional tests therefore narrow particular explanations rather than structurally excluding selection. The same qualification applies to the apparent decorrelation with discovery direction at large semimajor axis (step 017): a population transition in a proxy diagnostic, not a direct measurement of the radius of a field boundary. The characterized-survey literature supplies an essential opposing benchmark: simulated discovery selection can generate strong angular structure in intrinsically unclustered populations.

3.3 Survey-by-survey evidence

The resident ledger compares each survey cohort with its own adopted footprint baseline (step 113). SBDB has 26 of 44 objects in the longitude cap, compared with a baseline fraction near 0.42. DES has 13 of 16, compared with about 0.55. The broader OSSOS detached class has 13 of 31, compared with about 0.40, and is consistent with that baseline. These divergent outcomes are informative and are retained rather than averaged away.

3.4 Dynamical context

The pipeline examines element coupling, population selectivity, pole geometry and secular evolution (steps 011, 018–024, 066, 088 and 090). These tests constrain the particular dynamical realizations and initial conditions simulated. A lack of detected resonance structure in a small selected sample does not exclude every distant-planet population model. Likewise, a 20 Myr integration of the observed distribution is a stability experiment; it is not a comparison with a generative, survey-selected population evolved over Solar-System history.

The strongest resident result is therefore a measurable preferred sector under several explicit reference models. The remaining task is to determine whether the excess survives a survey simulator that includes the observed selection and quality cuts. This is a well-defined empirical target for any TEP field model.
