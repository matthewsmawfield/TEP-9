4. Transit geometry and the spatial aphelion channel

4.1 A directional population contrast

Among the 54 CODE-only deep-plunging class-1 comets, 19 lie in the principal three-dimensional cap. Their median original-to-future periapsis rotation is {{claim:code_in}}°, compared with {{claim:code_out}}° outside ($p =$ {{claim:code_p}}; step 034). The energy-kick comparison is not significant ($p =$ {{claim:energy_p}}). This is a directional contrast in the selected population's propagated orbital response; the nonsignificant energy comparison demonstrates neither zero exchange nor equivalence between energy distributions.

The Warsaw leg decomposition and the CODE comparison offer useful sample-level corroboration, while their common orbit-determination lineage limits methodological independence. Formal uncertainties, observational leverage, sky matching and published nongravitational solutions are examined in steps 058–060 and 079–082. These checks reduce specific confounds, but they do not propagate a full orbit covariance through the entire selection, integration and regression procedure, and pure-gravity fits do not establish the absence of physical cometary outgassing.

4.2 The full dynamical prediction and its reduced regression

The REBOUND/DE440s integrations reproduce the published original-to-future rotations closely (step 063). In the 54-object deep-plunger sample, the catalogue/simulation rank correlation is approximately 0.999 and the median absolute difference is about $6.4\times10^{-4}$ degrees. The direct catalogue-minus-simulation cap test gives $p =$ {{claim:direct_p}}. By contrast, regressing the simulated rotation on a few summary covariates gives a cap residual with $p =$ {{claim:proxy_p}}. These are different tests with different meanings.

Write the standard-dynamics rotation as $D_i^{\rm dyn}$, and a reduced regression on energy kick, sampled encounter distance, perihelion depth and inclination as $m(X_i)$. The statistic used in several pipeline steps is $$r_i^{\rm proxy}=\log D_i^{\rm dyn}-m(X_i).$$ The observation-minus-prediction comparison would instead involve $D_i^{\rm obs}-D_i^{\rm dyn}$ with a specified observational covariance. An anisotropy in $r_i^{\rm proxy}$ shows that these summary covariates do not remove all directional variation from the selected standard-dynamics population, yet it cannot establish that the full planetary calculation leaves the same amount of unexplained rotation.

The sampled minimum encounter distance is also an integration-output diagnostic, not necessarily the true continuous minimum of a close encounter. Boundary extraction, output cadence, nongravitational forces and covariance propagation require convergence checks before small per-object discrepancies can be interpreted. The catalogue agreement is valuable validation of the bulk machinery, but cannot replace those checks for a proposed new effect.

4.3 Equivalent orbital time is not a clock measurement

The pipeline converts an angular scale into years using $$\Delta t_{\rm eq}=\Delta\theta\,r_b^2/h,$$ where $h$ is specific angular momentum and $r_b$ a chosen reference radius (step 065). Since $h/r^2$ is the true-anomaly sweep rate, this defines an equivalent orbital phase timescale. A pure Keplerian phase advance changes the position along an orbit while leaving the eccentricity-vector direction unchanged. A periapsis-direction rotation and a phase advance therefore cannot be identified without an additional response calculation.

The reported “slip” maps, their harmonic fits and cross-validation scores describe this transformed statistic (steps 068, 071, 073, 075–076, 086, 089 and 102–106). They can be used to compare empirical profiles, provided the reference radius is fixed and the inherited regression assumptions are retained; they do not yet measure accumulated proper time, a lapse amplitude, or a spatially localized clock-rate discontinuity, and the factor $r_b^2$ must not be mistaken for independent evidence of a radial field law.

4.4 What the raw-astrometry refits add

The independent differential-correction implementation fits raw MPC astrometry and propagates the resulting trajectories under the same standard force model (steps 127–128 and 134). It avoids Warsaw fit products, but shares observations, a catalogue seed and a gravitational model with other constructions. It is a useful test of dependence on one orbit-fitting implementation; it is not an independent measurement of time.

The stored pre-2018 record contains 587 fitted comets. Its full-sample rotation-regression comparison has $p =$ {{claim:refit_total_p}}; its inbound carrier has $p =$ {{claim:refit_in_p}}; the independent-leg disagreement comparison has $p =$ {{claim:refit_leg_p}}. Reporting only the smallest carrier probability would conceal that the observable choice matters. The smaller historical signature refit also illustrates this sensitivity: for 17 usable objects, the one-sided Mann–Whitney test gives {{claim:lpc_refit_p}}, while the median-gap permutation gives {{claim:lpc_permutation_p}}. These test different distributional summaries.

The refits use a simplified astrometric response and clipping procedure, without a complete station/catalogue error model, orbit-covariance propagation, or a demonstrated detection efficiency for a specified TEP signal. Near-perfect correlation with an orbit seeded from the same observational record is not, on its own, certification that all reduction systematics are absent. The optimiser and cached results must be validated and versioned before very small fit-derived differences can be treated as precision evidence.

4.5 The temporal holdout fails the declared prediction

The primary post-2017 SBDB cohort was reserved relative to the historical CODE sample (step 117). Its 121 successful deep-plunger integrations give median rotation {{claim:holdout_in}}° inside the declared cap and {{claim:holdout_out}}° outside — the opposite of the predicted ordering. The prespecified greater-than comparison gives $p =$ {{claim:holdout_p}}, and the full reduced-regression comparison gives {{claim:holdout_resid_p}}. The 27 failed integrations among eligible primary objects enter the failure accounting; they are not silently treated as null outcomes.

This result does not validate the claim that the same fixed sector produces a stable positive rotation excess in new temporal cohorts. Later searches find a displaced modern direction and an era-dependent bipolar description (steps 118–123 and 125–133). Those analyses are useful for generating a revised hypothesis, but the new axis, epoch dependence and chosen morphology cannot retrospectively rescue the original held-out prediction. A revised model must be tested on new data.

4.6 The astrometric-catalogue control

Per-observation catalogue labels provide a direct test of a reduction-era confound (step 140). The modern displaced residual is nonsignificant in the uniform-Gaia stratum ($p =$ {{claim:gaia_p}}) and much stronger in the mixed-catalogue stratum ($p =$ {{claim:mixed_p}}). The legacy inbound carrier remains significant within its corresponding stratum ($p =$ {{claim:legacy_p}}). Uniform-Gaia denotes an adopted composition threshold, not a guarantee that every observation is Gaia-reduced.

Matching average catalogue composition does not exclude an interaction between catalogue, sky direction, epoch and observing station. The stratum contrast therefore weakens a universal displaced-field interpretation and motivates a catalogue-stitching explanation, without proving that explanation or establishing that the legacy signal is free of all catalogue errors. This control must accompany any bipolar or moving-boundary claim.

4.7 The spatial aphelion result remains a distinct target

The spatial aphelion statistic yields a more persistent result. On the SBDB record, the pre-2018 sample of 880 comets has $d_\parallel =$ {{claim:pre2018_dipole}}, and the post-2017 sample of 287 has {{claim:post2017_dipole}} (step 124). Under the latitude-conditioned Galactic reference, their finite-simulation probabilities are {{claim:pre2018_p}} and {{claim:post2017_p}}. The post-2017 ecliptic-latitude reference gives {{claim:post_ecliptic_p}}. The older sample reaches the 20,000-draw resolution floor.

Because it does not depend on a successful original-to-future integration for each object, this persistence is the clearest reason to retain a common spatial hypothesis despite the failed rotation holdout. The CometEls census and its magnitude, era and perihelion controls provide additional checks (steps 108–110), with overlapping membership explicitly relevant to their evidential weight. These catalogues remain selected samples: preservation of one latitude distribution is not proof that all angular visibility and discovery effects have been removed.

The nominal two-era Fisher combination is {{claim:era_fisher}} under independent, valid component nulls. The eras contain disjoint objects but share selection assumptions and an axis chosen from other observations; the combined number therefore summarizes the stated references rather than measuring a calibrated TEP probability, and the older era's Monte Carlo floor limits the precision of any combined tail estimate.
