Abstract

Building on the field detections and predictive clock-network results reported in the companion Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) papers, this paper investigates the field's outer-Solar-System structure. The approach compares resident trans-Neptunian objects with transiting long-period comets, using public catalogues, selection controls and numerical orbit propagation. A secure detached sample of 44 objects shows a perihelion-longitude concentration with resultant $R_\varpi =$ {{claim:resident_r}} ($p =$ {{claim:resident_p}} under an angularly randomized, orbit-conditioned null). In 54 CODE comets absent from the Warsaw sample, original-to-future periapsis rotations are {{claim:code_in}}° inside the resident-derived sector and {{claim:code_out}}° outside ($p =$ {{claim:code_p}}). These rotations are also reproduced by standard planetary dynamics; their directional regression residual is therefore a population statistic, not a direct failure of the dynamical model. The proposed equivalent-time conversion is not a measurement of proper time.

The clearest additional spatial result is an aphelion-direction dipole toward the same sector in both SBDB eras: $d_\parallel =$ {{claim:pre2018_dipole}} before 2018 and {{claim:post2017_dipole}} afterward. The latter survives both Galactic-latitude and ecliptic-latitude nulls ($p =$ {{claim:post2017_p}} and {{claim:post_ecliptic_p}}). In contrast, the temporally held-out comet cohort fails the predicted rotation excess. Subsequent bipolar geometry remains exploratory, and its modern residual is concentrated in mixed astrometric-catalogue observations. Within the imported TEP framework, the persistent spatial signal identifies a candidate domain structure. Establishing its geometry and response requires distinguishing boundary effects from selection and orbit reconstruction. Field existence is a premise supplied by the companion programme; the new inference addressed here is the outer-system boundary.
