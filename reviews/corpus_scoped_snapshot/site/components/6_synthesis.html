5. Synthesis and cross-channel constraints

5.1 An evidence ledger with distinct roles

Within the companion programme, the case for an outer-system TEP domain rests on the following conjunction: the resident directions identify a sector; a selected historical comet sample has an angular-response contrast there; and the aphelion spatial statistic persists across comet eras. The case is limited by a null direct catalogue-minus-dynamics contrast, failure of the temporal rotation prediction, overlapping catalogues and incomplete survey-selection modelling. A persuasive account must explain both sides of that ledger.

5.2 What a random-axis landscape measures

Step 126 combines ten directional summaries into a score. At the resident analysis axis its current value is {{claim:global_score}}, exceeded by a fraction {{claim:global_rank}} of 20,000 random directions. This describes how localized the observed score landscape is while the catalogues are held fixed and only the test direction changes. Because it does not generate new null datasets and repeat the entire axis-selection procedure, it is not a global look-elsewhere correction.

Several resident score terms first select objects inside an angular cap and then apply an unrestricted Rayleigh formula. Such selection itself concentrates angles, so those terms are descriptive weights rather than valid component probabilities. The catalogues also overlap, so evaluating them at shared directions preserves features of their observed correlation without making the ten terms independent or validating their nulls. A valid global test would simulate or resample under an explicitly justified null, preserve object identities and survey dependencies, repeat all fitting and searching, and compare the resulting maximum score with the observed one.

5.3 CMB-frame and mirror comparisons

The subsequent CMB-frame analysis examines opposite era dipoles, cone bands, a meridian plane and a reflected lobe (steps 129–135 and 139). The CMB direction is physically external to this comet dataset, but selecting which relation to test against it can still be retrospective. In particular, freezing a plane or mirror rule after inspecting these same directions does not create a prospective experiment.

The displaced direction lies about 11.5° from the equatorial mirror of the declared direction. Preserving its measured cone and randomizing its azimuth gives a tail of {{claim:mirror_p}}. The corrected joint meridian test jointly rotates the three directions of each ISO and excludes the resident direction that informed the sector; its conditional reference tail is {{claim:block_geometry_p}}. Neither reaches a 0.05 threshold. These calculations describe known geometry and do not include the full history of selecting the geometric hypothesis.

The GNSS-II direction is close to the proposed meridian, whereas the freely recovered LLR direction is far from it. A product of their plane-distance probabilities is not itself a joint p-value; the corresponding nominal Fisher reference is {{claim:meridian_p}}. The broader cross-channel Fisher calculation uses one GNSS-II statistic rather than counting that same direction twice, and reads the actual comet result instead of substituting a constant when a result key is missing. Its value, {{claim:cross_nominal_p}}, remains nominal: that arithmetic does not resolve shared GNSS observations, directional sensitivity, source-test calibration, or the retrospective choice of relations.

5.4 Spacecraft, plasma, nuclear and ISO channels

The spacecraft analyses use NAIF spacecraft-clock correlation kernels, Voyager plasma-wave data and RTG electrical-power records (steps 095–100, 103 and 137–141). NAIF SCLK coefficients map spacecraft clock counts to a reference time system. Changes in a piecewise clock-correlation solution cannot be identified directly with an anomalous oscillator frequency without modelling the correlation and operational procedures.

The signed crossing diagnostics contain negative fitted steps, but their comparison windows overlap and two crossings on one spacecraft are not independent experiments. Comparing a marked epoch with alternative epochs in the same time series gives a descriptive rank unless time-exchangeability and the selection of windows are justified. Independent-crossing binomial or Fisher references are therefore illustrative only. A cancellation property of a static propagation term in an idealized coherent two-way link does not automatically remove endpoint-clock, motion, calibration or time-dependent contributions from every measured channel.

Plasma-wave frequencies depend on electron density as well as any proposed measurement response; an environmental boundary is not independently a time-field boundary. RTG electrical power depends on thermoelectric conversion and aging as well as radioactive heat. The cross-generator control is valuable, but sibling units share bus, thermal and operational history. Converting electrical-power scatter into a constants or lapse bound requires explicit nuclear and engineering response assumptions; it cannot be called a direct Pu-238 clock measurement.

The three ISOs offer geometrical examples rather than a large statistical sample (step 101). Their nongravitational accelerations and explanatory classifications are heterogeneous, and a three-object classification match is sensitive to reclassifying one object. The ISO orbit directions are already represented as dependent groups in the geometric audit. No ISO geometry match is counted as an independent confirmation of TEP.

The solar-wind epoch analysis supplies a useful negative control (step 136): the pooled pressure association does not persist within the eras. Contemporaneous pressure at the adopted Earth-based proxy consequently does not support the proposed screening mechanism. This does not test every delayed, spatially transported heliospheric response, which would require its own specified model.
