2. Data, observables and analysis design

2.1 Catalogues and independence

The empirical measurements are public observations and published orbit solutions. Simulated null catalogues, orbit integrations, bootstrap samples and power calculations are computational controls; they are not additional observations. The JPL Small-Body Database (SBDB) supplies resident and cometary elements. Warsaw and CODE supply osculating heliocentric and original/future barycentric comet orbits. The one-apparition catalogue supplies another comet sample with published element uncertainties and comparison solutions. MPCORB, CometEls, DES and the characterized OSSOS ensemble support catalogue and discovery-selection checks.

Different catalogue names do not imply independent data. Warsaw, CODE and the one-apparition catalogue share a methodological lineage. SBDB and MPCORB often describe the same bodies, while separate orbit fits can reuse the same astrometry. DES provides an independent survey and reduction but overlaps the secure resident membership. In the ISO analysis, arrival, periapsis and departure directions belong to one orbit and must be handled as a correlated group.

2.2 What is actually measured

The perihelion-direction unit vector $\hat{\boldsymbol p}$ follows from inclination $i$, node $\Omega$ and argument of perihelion $\omega$. Longitude of perihelion $\varpi=\Omega+\omega$ is a circular angle; a cut on $\varpi$ is distinct from a three-dimensional cap on $\hat{\boldsymbol p}$. A uniform circular distribution places one third of its members within a 60° half-width, whereas an isotropic sphere places one quarter within a 60° cap. Each null must match the statistic actually used. Angular elements are not clock readings.

For comets, $d_{\rm of}=\angle(\hat{\boldsymbol p}_{\rm orig},\hat{\boldsymbol p}_{\rm fut})$ denotes the original-to-future periapsis angle, with analogous original-to-osculating and osculating-to-future angles. The published original and future solutions are obtained by dynamical propagation to the outer boundary; they are not independent observations at 250 AU (Królikowska 2014). Their agreement with a second integrator validates propagation. It does not, without an observation-level comparison, validate a new force or a time anomaly.

The spatial statistic is $d_\parallel=N^{-1}\sum_i\hat{\boldsymbol a}_i\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol n}$, where $\hat{\boldsymbol a}_i=-\hat{\boldsymbol p}_i$ is the aphelion direction and $\hat{\boldsymbol n}$ the reference axis. This avoids differencing propagated boundary legs. It remains a quantity inferred from fitted orbital elements, subject to discovery selection and astrometric error: “spatial” does not mean independent of reconstruction.

2.3 Frames, numerical precision and nulls

Orbital directions are expressed in the ecliptic J2000 frame. The shared transformation to Galactic coordinates first applies the J2000 obliquity rotation to equatorial coordinates, then the equatorial-to-Galactic matrix. Directly applying an equatorial-to-Galactic matrix to ecliptic vectors is incorrect. The adopted transform is checked against Astropy and the SPICE frame convention. The resident and transit axes have different Galactic coordinates; the transit axis is approximately $(166^\circ,-54^\circ)$.

The conditioned resident null holds $(a,e,i)$ fixed and randomizes the angular orientations. Footprint controls add assumptions about discovery longitude and its coupling to perihelion direction. Galactic-latitude and ecliptic-latitude comet nulls preserve selected latitude information while randomizing other orientation components. These are explicit and useful reference models, but neither substitutes for a complete population-plus-survey simulation.

Finite Monte Carlo tests use $p=(k+1)/(B+1)$, with $k$ null statistics at least as extreme as the observed statistic among $B$ simulations. Zero exceedances in 20,000 draws correspond to a reported rank of $1/20001$, not a measured zero probability. Full precision is retained before combining tests. Historical result files include varied reporting conventions; only explicitly bound result leaves are used for the main quantitative claims.
