7. Conclusions

Building on the companion TEP field-detection and predictive-clock programme, this paper identifies a preferred resident sector and a persistent comet-aphelion dipole in the outer Solar System under the tested angular nulls. Their common direction is the principal evidence for a shared domain structure. The field's existence and matter coupling are inherited premises; the new question is the location, geometry and observable response of that domain.

The temporal rotation holdout fails the original fixed-sector excess prediction, while the later displaced structure depends on astrometric-catalogue composition. These findings constrain the proposed outer-system response. Historical rotations are reproduced by conventional propagation, so attribution to TEP requires carrying the field signal through the orbit fit, as the companion pulsar analysis illustrates. Catalogue overlap and shared reduction procedures remain relevant to the boundary inference.

The conformal matter action provides a concrete dynamical connection: a spatial change in the clock factor produces a linked trajectory response. The derived planar limit and its numerical controls supply a starting point for that calculation. The persistent spatial evidence, imported companion constraints and coupled response define the case to be completed: a common field configuration that predicts the resident structure and fitted comet observations with shared parameters. A specific outer-system boundary is established by that quantitative agreement, rather than by treating the catalogue as a new standalone vote on TEP's existence.
