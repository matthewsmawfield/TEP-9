9. Data availability and reproducibility

9.1 Data availability

All catalogues used are public. The JPL Small-Body Database is queried live through https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/sbdb_query.api. The OSSOS characterized ensemble, the Warsaw comet-orbit tables, and the one-apparition comet catalogue are obtained from the CDS/VizieR archives (J/ApJS/236/18, J/A+A/567/A126, and J/A+A/571/A63). The CODE catalogue is distributed through the Poznań comet-dynamics server at pad2.astro.amu.edu.pl; the copy analysed here is an archival snapshot of the published table, integrity-pinned by SHA-256 in data/raw/code/provenance.json. MPCORB and the JPL CometEls file complete the auxiliary data; the N-body baseline draws planet states from the JPL DE440s ephemeris kernel (data/raw/spice/de440s.bsp, SHA-256 pinned in provenance). No synthetic or simulated data enter any reported statistic.

The traceability claim is itself audited mechanically (step 114): a registered pipeline step parses these components, resolves every cited step, result handle, and figure file, and matches every numeric literal in step-citing paragraphs against the numeric leaves of the results files. On the present manuscript, 2,943 literals are checked: 1,712 trace to a numeric leaf inside the cited steps' own result files (strict provenance), a further 1,171 match only against the full results universe — a coincidence-prone pool enumerated separately and not counted as provenance — and 60 match neither and are enumerated in the audit ledger for review (years, section numbers, isotope labels, order-of-magnitude anchors, and literature values). Zero cited steps, result handles, or figures are unresolvable, and no result file is stale. The audit is re-run on every pipeline execution, so any number that drifts from its source is flagged, not silently inherited.

9.4 Known limitations of the data record

Three limitations are carried explicitly. The CODE acquisition is an archival snapshot rather than a live export — the pad2 interface did not return a machine-readable table at retrieval — though its content is the published catalogue and its hash is pinned. Warsaw, CODE, and the one-apparition catalogue share orbit-determination lineage, so the transit replications are independent in sample, not method; no fully independent three-leg lineage exists in public form, the nearest approach being the embedded Marsden–Williams (2008) comparison series tested in step 060. And the N-body planetary baseline, though validated end-to-end against the catalogue's own energy kicks, is a nominal-element integration: in the deeply chaotic encounter tail, individual kicks are unstable and the baseline functions as a statistical control rather than a per-comet reconstruction.
